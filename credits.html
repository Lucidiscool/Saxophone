This project celebrates the saxophone family with interactive 3D studies, open photography, and sampled sound.

3D model & rendering

Alto saxophone owned and played by Charlie Parker, Smithsonian National Museum of African American History and Culture, object 2019.10.1a-g. King Super 20, circa 1947. Model provided through Smithsonian Open Access; the object page identifies this media as public domain. Open Access information.

The scan has been reoriented and scaled for the studio, with adjusted lighting and added interactive key targets. Desktop uses the 4K texture derivative; mobile uses the 2K derivative. The geometry and surface detail come from the real instrument. Click targets use a simplified one-note mapping, not authentic saxophone fingerings. No Smithsonian endorsement is implied.

Rendered with Three.js 0.186.1, including OrbitControls, RoomEnvironment, and GLTFLoader, under the MIT license. Draco geometry decoding is distributed under the Apache 2.0 license.

The sound

FluidR3 GM saxophone samples, rendered by MIDI.js Soundfonts / gleitz, provided under CC BY 3.0 as documented by that project. Samples are played at adjusted pitches. The studio currently plays only the alto. Its sound is synthesized from these samples and is not a recording of Charlie Parker or his instrument.

Tap a model key for a short note. Hold a piano key to play; release it to stop. Sample recordings have a finite length. Written notes span C4–C6 and sound at the selected instrument’s transposition. Rare experimental instruments vary in design and tuning.

Further reading
